In a cashless claim, your insurer pays the network hospital directly after approving your treatment, so you pay only for items the policy does not cover. In a reimbursement claim, you pay the hospital yourself and then send the bills to the insurer to get your money back. Cashless is usually easier, but it works only at hospitals in your insurer's network. Knowing both routes helps you avoid delays and disputes.

## Key takeaways

- Cashless claims need pre-authorisation from the insurer or its third-party administrator (TPA) before or soon after admission.
- IRDAI rules require insurers to decide on cashless requests within one hour and to approve final discharge within three hours of the hospital's request.
- Reimbursement claims need original bills, reports and a discharge summary, and should be filed within the time stated in your policy.
- Non-disclosure, waiting periods, exclusions and missing documents are the most common reasons for rejection.
- If you disagree with a decision, complain to the insurer first, then use Bima Bharosa or approach the Insurance Ombudsman.

## Cashless and reimbursement at a glance

| | Cashless | Reimbursement |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital | Network hospital only | Any qualifying hospital |
| Who pays the bill | Insurer pays the hospital directly | You pay first, insurer repays you |
| Approval | Pre-authorisation needed | Claim reviewed after treatment |
| Your out-of-pocket cost | Only non-payable items, co-pay or deductibles | Full bill upfront until reimbursed |
| Paperwork | Mostly handled by hospital | You collect and submit documents |

## How a cashless claim works

### Planned hospitalisation

For planned surgery or treatment, inform your insurer or TPA in advance. Many policies ask for notice a few days before admission, so check the timeline in your policy wording.

1. Confirm that the hospital is in your insurer's network.
2. At the hospital's insurance desk, show your health card or policy number and a photo ID.
3. The hospital fills in the pre-authorisation form with your doctor's diagnosis and estimated cost, and sends it to the insurer.
4. The insurer approves, asks for more details, or declines the request.
5. At discharge, the hospital sends the final bill for approval. You pay only for non-covered items.

### Emergency hospitalisation

In an emergency, get treatment first. The hospital or a family member should inform the insurer as soon as possible, usually within the time limit in your policy, such as 24 hours of admission. The pre-authorisation process then runs in the same way.

### IRDAI timelines for cashless claims

Under IRDAI's 2024 Master Circular on health insurance, insurers must decide on a cashless authorisation request immediately, and in any case within one hour of receiving it. Final authorisation at discharge must be given within three hours of the hospital's request.

If the final approval takes longer than three hours, any extra charges the hospital levies for the delay are to be borne by the insurer, not by you. The circular also says that if a patient dies during treatment, the insurer must process the claim at once so that the body can be released without delay.

### If cashless is declined

A cashless denial is not always a final rejection of the claim. It may happen because information was incomplete or the treatment needs further review. You can pay the hospital and file a reimbursement claim with full documents, and the insurer will assess it again.

## How a reimbursement claim works

### When you will need it

Reimbursement applies when you are treated at a non-network hospital, when cashless approval was not obtained or was declined, or for expenses like pre- and post-hospitalisation costs that some policies settle separately.

### Step-by-step process

1. Inform the insurer about the hospitalisation within the time set in your policy.
2. Pay the hospital and collect all original bills and reports at discharge.
3. Fill in the claim form, available from the insurer's website or app.
4. Submit the claim form and documents within the deadline in your policy, often within a few weeks of discharge.
5. Respond quickly to any request for additional information.
6. Track the claim status online or through customer care.

### Documents checklist

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Completed claim form | Signed by the policyholder |
| Discharge summary | Shows diagnosis, treatment and dates |
| Final hospital bill with itemised breakup | Original, with payment receipt |
| Pharmacy bills | Backed by doctor's prescriptions |
| Investigation reports | Blood tests, scans, X-rays and so on |
| Doctor's prescription and consultation notes | Including first consultation for the illness |
| Policy copy or health card and ID proof | KYC as required by the insurer |
| Cancelled cheque or bank details | For payment of the claim |
| Police report or medico-legal certificate | Only for accidents, where applicable |

Keep photocopies or scans of everything you send. The 2024 Master Circular asks insurers and TPAs to collect required documents directly from hospitals wherever possible, which should reduce the burden on patients, particularly for cashless claims.

### How long settlement takes

Insurers are generally required to settle or reject a claim within 30 days of receiving the last necessary document. If payment is delayed beyond the allowed time, the policyholder is entitled to interest at 2% above the bank rate. Timelines can be longer if the insurer formally investigates the claim.

## Common reasons claims are rejected or reduced

- **Non-disclosure:** a pre-existing condition or habit was not declared on the proposal form.
- **Waiting periods:** treatment was for a condition still within its waiting period.
- **Exclusions:** the treatment is excluded, such as most cosmetic procedures.
- **Late intimation or filing:** the insurer was not informed, or documents were not submitted, within the policy deadlines.
- **Missing or inconsistent documents:** for example, bills without a prescription.
- **No need for admission:** the insurer decides the treatment could have been done on an outpatient basis.
- **Policy limits:** room-rent caps, sub-limits or co-pay reduce the amount payable.

Under the 2024 Master Circular, a claim can be rejected only with the approval of the insurer's claims review committee, and the insurer must cite the specific policy terms behind a rejection or partial payment. Ask for this in writing if it is not provided.

## What to do if you disagree with a decision

### Step 1: Complain to the insurer

Write to the insurer's grievance redressal officer (GRO), whose details appear in your policy documents and on the insurer's website. Explain the issue clearly, include your policy and claim numbers, and attach supporting papers.

### Step 2: Escalate through Bima Bharosa

If the insurer does not resolve your complaint, register it on Bima Bharosa, IRDAI's grievance portal. You can track the status online. The portal states that it never asks complainants for payment, so beware of anyone who does.

### Step 3: Approach the Insurance Ombudsman

If the insurer does not reply within one month, or you are unhappy with its reply, you can approach the Insurance Ombudsman. The complaint must be filed within one year of the insurer's final reply, and the amount claimed must not exceed ₹50 lakh. Filing is free and can be done online through the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen website. Insurers that do not implement an Ombudsman award within 30 days must pay the complainant ₹5,000 for each day of delay.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get cashless treatment at any hospital?

No. Cashless treatment is available only at hospitals in your insurer's network. At other hospitals you will usually need to pay and claim reimbursement. Check the network list on your insurer's website before planned treatment.

### Do I need to inform the insurer in an emergency?

Yes, but treatment comes first. Inform the insurer or TPA as soon as practical, within the time your policy specifies. Late intimation is a common avoidable reason for disputes.

### Why was my claim paid only partly?

Partial payments usually result from room-rent caps, sub-limits, co-pay, deductibles or items the policy lists as non-payable, such as some consumables. The settlement letter should explain each deduction. If it does not, ask the insurer for a written breakup.

### Can I claim from two policies for one hospital bill?

Yes. If you have more than one health policy, you can choose which policy to claim from first. If the first claim does not cover the full admissible amount, the balance can be claimed from the other policy.

### Does a rejected cashless request mean my claim is lost?

Not necessarily. You can pay the hospital and file a reimbursement claim with full documents, and the insurer will review it again. If you still disagree, use the grievance steps above.
