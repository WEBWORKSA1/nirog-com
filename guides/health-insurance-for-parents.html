Health Insurance for Parents and Senior Citizens: What Actually Matters

Health insurance for parents and senior citizens in India: pre-existing diseases, waiting periods, co-pay, PM-JAY 70+ cover and higher tax benefits.

By the Nirog editorial teamFact-checked against the sources listed belowUpdated 28 October 20268 min read

The best health insurance for parents covers their existing conditions as early as possible, has little or no co-pay, avoids room-rent and disease-wise caps, and has a sum insured large enough for a serious hospital stay. Older buyers can now get cover at any age, and every Indian aged 70 or above is eligible for free government cover under PM-JAY. Most families do best by combining these options rather than relying on one.

Key takeaways

  • There is no longer an upper age limit for buying health insurance, so it is never too late to apply.
  • Pre-existing diseases must be covered after a waiting period of no more than three years, and some plans offer shorter periods.
  • Co-pay and sub-limits are the features that most often surprise families at claim time.
  • All citizens aged 70 and above can get up to ₹5 lakh a year of free hospital cover through the Ayushman Vay Vandana card, regardless of income.
  • Premiums you pay for parents can earn a higher tax deduction when they are senior citizens, under the old tax regime.

Why buying for parents is different

Older adults are more likely to need hospital care, and many already live with conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease or arthritis. That changes what matters in a policy. A cheap plan that excludes or caps treatment for these conditions may offer little real protection.

Premiums also rise with age. Since January 2025, IRDAI has required insurers to keep yearly premium increases for senior citizens within 10%, unless they first consult the regulator. This makes long-term costs more predictable, though premiums will still climb over time.

Pre-existing diseases and waiting periods

What counts as pre-existing

A pre-existing disease is generally any condition diagnosed, treated or showing symptoms within a set period before you buy the policy. Common examples include diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disorders and earlier heart problems.

How long you wait

Since April 2024, the waiting period for pre-existing diseases can be no longer than three years. Many plans offer shorter periods, and some let you reduce the wait by paying an extra premium. During this time, hospitalisation linked to that condition is not covered.

Other waiting periods still apply. Most policies have a 30-day initial wait for illness, and a separate wait for specific listed treatments such as cataract or joint replacement, which are common in older age.

Waiting period type Typical rule Why it matters for parents
Initial 30 days for illness; accidents usually covered Do not delay buying
Specific diseases Set period for listed conditions Cataract and knee surgery are often on this list
Pre-existing diseases Maximum 3 years Diabetes and heart disease covered only after this

Honest disclosure protects your claim

Declare every known condition, medicine and past surgery on the proposal form. Hiding a condition can lead to a claim being rejected. After 60 months of continuous cover, the moratorium rule stops the insurer from disputing claims for non-disclosure, except in cases of proven fraud.

Features to check in a senior citizen policy

Co-payment

Many senior plans require you to pay a share of each claim, often 10% to 30%. On a ₹4 lakh bill, a 20% co-pay means ₹80,000 from your pocket. Plans without co-pay cost more but are often worth it for older parents.

Room-rent caps and sub-limits

A daily room-rent cap can trigger proportionate cuts on the whole bill if a costlier room is used. Sub-limits on procedures common in older age, such as cataract surgery or joint replacement, can also leave large gaps.

Sum insured

Choose cover for a major event like cardiac surgery or cancer treatment in the hospitals your parents would actually use. If the base premium is high, a super top-up can add cover at a lower cost.

Day care, domiciliary and AYUSH cover

Many modern treatments, such as chemotherapy sessions or cataract surgery, do not need a 24-hour stay. Check that day-care procedures are covered. Domiciliary treatment and AYUSH treatment cover can also help, depending on your parents' preferences.

Feature What to prefer Watch out for
Co-pay None or low Zone-based co-pay in some cities
Room rent No cap or any single private room Percentage-of-sum-insured caps
Sub-limits None or few Caps on cataract and joint replacement
Network hospitals Hospitals near your parents Network that excludes local hospitals
Renewal Lifelong renewability Unclear renewal terms

Medical tests before purchase

Insurers often ask older applicants to undergo a pre-policy health check. This may include blood sugar, blood pressure, lipid profile, kidney and liver function tests, an ECG and sometimes more, depending on age and sum insured.

Do not see these tests as a barrier. They create a clear record of health at the time of purchase, which can reduce disputes later. Ask who pays for the tests, as practice varies between insurers. If results show a condition, the insurer may still offer cover with a waiting period, a loading on premium or a specific exclusion. Read the offer letter carefully before accepting.

Government cover for seniors 70 and above

Who qualifies

Since late 2024, every Indian citizen aged 70 or above can get health cover under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY, regardless of income. They receive a dedicated Ayushman Vay Vandana card.

What it covers

The card offers free cashless treatment of up to ₹5 lakh a year at empanelled public and private hospitals, covering a wide range of procedures. Pre-existing conditions are covered from day one. If the senior already belongs to a PM-JAY family, they get an additional top-up of up to ₹5 lakh a year that is for them alone.

How it fits with private insurance

Seniors who already have private health insurance can still use PM-JAY. Those covered by CGHS, ECHS or CAPF health schemes must choose between their existing scheme and PM-JAY. Enrolment is possible through the Ayushman app, the official beneficiary portal or at an empanelled hospital. For queries, the National Health Authority helpline is 14555.

PM-JAY works best as a safety net. A private policy may still help with access to non-empanelled hospitals or treatments outside the PM-JAY package list.

Tax benefits for parents' premiums

If you pay your parents' health insurance premium and use the old tax regime, you can claim a deduction. From the 2026-27 tax year, this falls under Section 126 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which replaced the earlier Section 80D.

Insured Maximum deduction
Parents below 60 ₹25,000
Parents, at least one a senior citizen ₹50,000
Senior citizen parents with no insurance Up to ₹50,000 of medical expenses
Preventive check-ups Up to ₹5,000 within the above limits

This is in addition to the deduction for your own family's cover. Premiums must be paid by a non-cash method.

Frequently asked questions

Can my 75-year-old parent still buy health insurance?

Yes. IRDAI removed the upper age limit for buying health insurance in April 2024. Expect higher premiums, a possible medical check-up and, in some plans, a co-pay. Your parent is also eligible for PM-JAY cover as they are above 70.

Will a policy cover my father's diabetes?

Usually yes, after the pre-existing disease waiting period, which can be no more than three years. Some plans offer a shorter wait or special cover for diabetes from an earlier date. Check the policy wording for the exact terms.

Is a co-pay plan a bad idea?

Not always. A co-pay plan can make cover affordable when the alternative is no insurance at all. Just make sure you can comfortably pay your share of a large claim.

Can I port my parents' existing policy to a better one?

Yes, you can port at renewal and keep credit for waiting periods already served. The new insurer may assess your parents afresh and can accept or decline the request. Start the process well before the renewal date.

Does the Ayushman Vay Vandana card replace private insurance?

It provides valuable free cover, but it is limited to empanelled hospitals and listed procedures. Many families keep a private policy alongside it for wider choice.
