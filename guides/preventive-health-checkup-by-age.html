Preventive Health Check-ups by Age: Which Tests to Do and When

A decade-by-decade guide to preventive health check-ups in India, what each test in a full-body package means, and how to claim the ₹5,000 tax deduction.

By the Nirog editorial teamFact-checked against the sources listed belowUpdated 28 October 20269 min read

A preventive health check-up is a set of tests done when you feel well, so that silent problems such as high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol or early cancer are caught before they cause harm. Most adults in India should start basic checks in their 20s and add more tests with each decade. The right list depends on your age, sex, family history and lifestyle, so use this guide as a starting point for a conversation with your doctor.

Key takeaways

  • Blood pressure, weight, waist size, blood sugar and cholesterol are the core checks for almost every adult.
  • Indians tend to develop diabetes and heart disease earlier and at lower body weight, so screening should not wait until your 50s.
  • Cancer screening for women (cervical and breast) and the PSA discussion for men are age-specific and should be planned with a doctor.
  • More tests are not always better. Choose a package based on your risk, not on how many parameters it lists.
  • Payments for a preventive check-up can be deducted up to ₹5,000 a year within the health insurance deduction limit, under the old tax regime.

Why check-ups matter when you feel fine

Many of the conditions that shorten lives in India give no warning for years. A large national survey by ICMR found that about 11.4% of adults have diabetes, 15.3% have prediabetes and 35.5% have high blood pressure. A large share of these people do not know it.

Finding a problem early usually means simpler treatment, lower costs and fewer complications.

The government's NCD programme now offers screening for high blood pressure, diabetes and oral, breast and cervical cancer to adults aged 30 and above, including at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs. If cost is a barrier, ask at your nearest centre.

A note on HbA1c numbers

According to the American Diabetes Association, an HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.4% indicates prediabetes and 6.5% or higher suggests diabetes. A fasting glucose of 100 to 125 mg/dL is the prediabetes range, and 126 mg/dL or more suggests diabetes. A diagnosis is normally confirmed with a repeat test.

Check-ups decade by decade

In your 20s

Build a baseline. Record your weight, BMI, waist circumference and blood pressure. A CBC, fasting glucose or HbA1c and a lipid profile are reasonable once, and earlier if you have a family history of diabetes or early heart disease.

Women should discuss the HPV vaccine with their doctor.

In your 30s

Add regular blood pressure and blood sugar checks. The ADA advises testing all adults for diabetes from age 35, and earlier for Asian adults with a BMI of 23 or more plus other risk factors. If results are normal, repeating at least every three years is common practice; with prediabetes, yearly testing is usual.

The WHO recommends that women begin cervical screening with an HPV test from age 30, repeated every 5 to 10 years. A Pap smear is still widely used in India where HPV testing is not available.

In your 40s

This is when heart and metabolic risks rise sharply for many Indians. Plan a fuller check with lipids, sugar, kidney and liver tests, plus an ECG if advised.

The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) now recommends a mammogram every two years for women from 40 to 74. Indian practice varies, so discuss timing with your doctor, especially if a close relative had breast cancer.

In your 50s

Continue the core tests, usually yearly. Ask about colorectal cancer screening, bone health (especially for women after menopause), and hearing and eye checks, including glaucoma.

For men aged 55 to 69, the USPSTF says PSA testing for prostate cancer should be an individual decision. PSA can pick up cancers early, but it can also lead to false alarms, unneeded biopsies and treatment of slow cancers that would never have caused harm. Talk through the pros and cons first.

At 60 and beyond

Along with yearly sugar, BP, lipid and kidney checks, ask about bone density, vision, hearing, memory, falls risk, a review of your medicines, and influenza and pneumococcal vaccines.

The USPSTF advises against routine PSA screening from age 70, because the harms tend to outweigh the benefits.

Men and women: what differs

Women need cervical and breast cancer screening and a haemoglobin check, since anaemia is very common. Thyroid problems are also more frequent in women.

Men tend to develop heart disease earlier. Waist size is a useful red flag: for Asian Indians, a waist of 90 cm or more in men and 80 cm or more in women signals abdominal obesity. Men should also have the PSA conversation at the right age.

Tax benefit: the ₹5,000 preventive check-up deduction

Under Section 80D of the Income-tax Act, 1961, money spent on a preventive health check-up for yourself, your spouse, dependent children or parents can be deducted up to ₹5,000 in total each year. This is not an extra allowance. It sits inside the overall 80D limit of ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if the person covered is a senior citizen).

Check-up fees can be paid in cash, unlike insurance premiums, and the deduction is available only under the old tax regime.

From tax year 2026-27, the new Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the old law, and the same benefit appears under Section 126 with the ₹5,000 check-up sub-limit retained. Keep your bills and confirm the details with a tax professional before filing.

When to see a doctor

Do not wait for your annual check-up if you notice:

  • Unexplained weight loss, constant thirst or frequent urination
  • Chest pain, breathlessness or palpitations
  • A new lump in the breast, neck or elsewhere
  • Bleeding between periods, after menopause or after sex
  • Blood in urine or stool, or a mouth ulcer that does not heal

For sudden chest pain, signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech) or collapse, call 108 or 112 immediately.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I get a full-body check-up?

For a healthy adult under 40 with normal results, every one to three years is usually enough. After 40, or if you have risk factors such as diabetes in the family, a yearly check is sensible. Your doctor may advise a different interval based on your results.

Do I need to fast before a health check-up?

Fasting glucose and some lipid tests need 8 to 12 hours without food, though water is fine. HbA1c does not need fasting. Ask the lab for instructions when you book.

Can I claim the ₹5,000 deduction for my parents' check-up?

Yes. Check-ups for parents can be included, but the ₹5,000 cap applies to the combined total for self, family and parents. It also counts within the overall 80D or Section 126 limits.

Should young women get a Pap smear or HPV test?

The WHO recommends starting cervical screening at 30 for most women, using an HPV test where available. Earlier or more frequent screening may be advised for women living with HIV. The HPV vaccine works best when given at 9 to 14 years of age.
