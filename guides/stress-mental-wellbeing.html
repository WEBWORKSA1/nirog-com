Stress and Mental Well-being: Everyday Habits That Protect Your Mind

Protect your mental well-being with everyday habits for stress: sleep, movement, digital balance, talking to someone, Tele-MANAS 14416 and when to get help.

By the Nirog editorial teamFact-checked against the sources listed belowUpdated 28 October 20268 min read

Stress is a normal reaction to pressure, and small daily habits such as regular sleep, physical activity, time away from screens and talking to people you trust can stop it from building up. When worry or low mood lasts for weeks and starts affecting work, sleep or relationships, it may be more than ordinary stress. In India, free and confidential support is available 24 hours a day through the Tele-MANAS helpline on 14416.

Key takeaways

  • Stress is a natural response to challenges; it becomes a problem when it is constant and starts to affect daily life.
  • Anxiety disorders and depression are health conditions, not weaknesses, and they respond well to treatment.
  • Consistent sleep, regular movement, balanced meals and staying connected are among the most reliable ways to protect mental health.
  • Tele-MANAS (14416 or 1-800-891-4416) offers free mental health support in about 20 languages, around the clock.
  • Under the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017, insurers must cover mental illness on the same basis as physical illness.

Stress, anxiety and depression: what is the difference?

Stress

The World Health Organization describes stress as worry or mental tension caused by a difficult situation. Everyone feels it at times, whether from exams, work deadlines, money worries or caring for a family member. Short bursts of stress can even help you focus. It usually eases once the situation passes.

Anxiety

Anxiety becomes a disorder when fear and worry are intense, hard to control and out of proportion to the situation. It often comes with physical signs such as a racing heart, sweating, trembling, stomach upset, restlessness and poor sleep. People may start avoiding places or situations that trigger these feelings.

Depression

Depression involves a low, sad or empty mood, or a loss of interest in things you used to enjoy, for most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks. Other signs include tiredness, poor concentration, changes in sleep or appetite, feelings of guilt or worthlessness, and hopelessness.

Main feeling Pressure, tension Excessive fear or worry Low mood, loss of interest
Usual trigger A specific situation Often out of proportion or unclear May have no clear trigger

This table is a simple guide, not a diagnostic tool. Only a qualified professional can assess whether someone has a mental health condition.

Everyday habits that protect your mind

Keep a steady routine

A predictable daily structure for meals, work, rest and family time can help you feel more in control. When life feels chaotic, even small anchors, like a fixed wake-up time or an evening walk, can make a difference.

Prioritise sleep

Aim to go to bed and wake up at the same time each day, including weekends. Keep your bedroom dark, quiet and comfortable. Avoid heavy meals, caffeine and alcohol late in the evening, and put screens away for some time before bed.

Move your body

Regular physical activity is one of the best-studied ways to reduce stress and lift mood. You do not need a gym. A brisk walk, yoga, cycling, dancing or playing a sport all count. Start with what you can manage and build up gradually.

Eat regular, balanced meals

Skipping meals and relying on sugary snacks can leave you tired and irritable. Regular meals with vegetables, fruit, whole grains, pulses and enough water support steady energy. Limiting alcohol matters too, since it can worsen anxiety and low mood.

Try simple calming techniques

Slow breathing, mindfulness and grounding exercises can help during tense moments. One approach is to pause, notice your thoughts and feelings without judging them, and then gently bring your attention back to what you are doing. The WHO's free guide Doing What Matters in Times of Stress teaches such skills in a few minutes a day.

Make time for things you enjoy

Hobbies, music, prayer, gardening, cooking or time outdoors are not luxuries. They give your mind a break and help you recover from pressure.

Digital well-being

Phones and social media keep us connected, but constant notifications, late-night scrolling and comparison with others can raise stress. A few practical limits help:

  • Turn off non-essential notifications and check messages at set times.
  • Keep phones out of the bedroom, or at least away from the bed.
  • Limit news and social media if they leave you anxious, and choose trusted sources.
  • Notice how certain apps make you feel, and mute or unfollow accounts that drain you.
  • Plan screen-free time with family, such as during meals.

Scrolling in bed Reading, light stretching or slow breathing
Checking news many times a day One or two set times from a trusted source

Talking to someone helps

Many people in India hesitate to talk about their mental health because of stigma or fear of being judged. Yet sharing what you feel with someone you trust, such as a friend, family member, teacher or colleague, can ease the burden and help you see options more clearly.

Professional help is also widely available. A family doctor can be a good first step and can refer you to a psychiatrist, psychologist or counsellor. Seeking help early often means problems are easier to manage.

Tele-MANAS

Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's national tele-mental health service, launched in October 2022. You can call 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, free of charge, at any time of day or night. Trained counsellors offer support in about 20 Indian languages and can connect callers to specialists or local services when needed. A Tele-MANAS mobile app also offers self-care resources.

Mental health and health insurance

The Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 requires every insurer to provide medical insurance for the treatment of mental illness on the same basis as cover for physical illness. Following the Act, IRDAI directed insurers in 2018 to include mental illness in their health policies.

In practice, this usually means hospitalisation for a mental health condition is covered on the same terms as other illnesses, subject to the policy's waiting periods and conditions. Outpatient therapy and counselling sessions may or may not be covered, depending on the plan. Read your policy wording or ask your insurer exactly what is included.

When to seek help

Reach out to a doctor, counsellor or Tele-MANAS (14416 or 1-800-891-4416) if:

  • Low mood, worry or irritability lasts more than two weeks.
  • You have trouble sleeping, eating or concentrating most days.
  • Stress is affecting your work, studies or relationships.
  • You are using alcohol, tobacco or other substances to cope.
  • You feel hopeless, numb or unable to enjoy anything.
  • You have panic attacks or avoid everyday situations because of fear.

If you or someone you know is thinking about self-harm or suicide, or is in immediate danger, call the national emergency number 112 right away or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. Do not leave the person alone if it is safe to stay with them.

Frequently asked questions

Is stress always bad for you?

No. Short-term stress can help you focus and respond to challenges. Problems arise when stress is intense, constant or long-lasting and you have little chance to recover. That is when it can affect sleep, mood and physical health.

How do I know if I need professional help?

If distressing feelings last more than a couple of weeks or interfere with daily life, it is a good idea to speak to a professional. You do not need to wait for a crisis. Early support is often simpler and more effective.

Is Tele-MANAS really free and confidential?

Yes. Tele-MANAS is a free government service available 24 hours a day on 14416 or 1-800-891-4416. Calls are handled by trained mental health professionals and counsellors in many Indian languages.

Does health insurance cover mental illness in India?

Under the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017, insurers must cover mental illness on the same basis as physical illness. Hospitalisation is generally covered, subject to policy terms. Check your policy wording for details on outpatient therapy.

Can exercise really help with anxiety and low mood?

Yes. Regular physical activity is linked to lower stress and better mood, and it improves sleep. It works best alongside other healthy habits and, where needed, professional treatment rather than as a replacement for it.
