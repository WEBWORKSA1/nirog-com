How it works

Body mass index (BMI) is your weight in kilograms divided by the square of your height in metres. It is a quick screening number, not a diagnosis.

For South Asians, Indian consensus guidelines and the WHO Asia-Pacific expert group recommend lower cut-offs than the global ones: 18.5–22.9 is healthy, 23–24.9 is overweight and 25 or more is obesity. That is because people of Indian origin tend to carry more fat around the abdomen and develop type 2 diabetes and heart disease at lower body weights.

BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so pair it with a waist measurement. A waist over 90 cm in men or 80 cm in women signals extra risk even when BMI looks fine.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI for Indians?

For adults of Indian origin, a BMI between 18.5 and 22.9 is considered healthy. Overweight starts at 23 and obesity at 25, which is lower than the global cut-offs of 25 and 30.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

No. Muscle is denser than fat, so muscular people can have a high BMI without excess body fat. Waist-to-height ratio or a body-fat test gives a better picture.

Does BMI work for children?

Children need age- and sex-specific BMI percentile charts. This calculator is for adults aged 18 and over.

Note: This tool gives general estimates for healthy adults. It is not medical advice or a diagnosis. Talk to a doctor about your own results.
