# Nirog.com — Competitive review (36 sites)

**Date:** 2026-10-28

**Summary.** We looked at 40 health and wellness properties: the 36 in the brief plus four extras (nih.gov, heart.org, diabetes.org and calculator.net's BMI page). They fall into five groups: Western publishers (Healthline, WebMD, Medical News Today and similar), public health portals (NHS, MedlinePlus, CDC, WHO, healthdirect), hospital systems (Mayo, Cleveland, Hopkins, Medanta, Max), Indian health-commerce apps (1mg, PharmEasy, Apollo 247, Practo, Netmeds) and Indian insurance brokers (Policybazaar, ACKO, Ditto, Coverfox). Four sites blocked or returned nothing when fetched: Verywell Health, Everyday Health, OnlyMyHealth and cult.fit. Those rows use what is publicly known, and Fitbit redirected to the Google Store. The winning sites share three things. They put trust signals right on the page: named reviewers, editorial policies, cited sources and certification badges. They build a set of free tools that people come back to: calculators, checkers and risk tests. They also turn readers into leads with short, staged forms that state their privacy promises clearly. No site combines Western-grade E-E-A-T, Hindi and regional-language depth, and neutral preventive-health lead gen in one place. Indian publishers are thin on trust. Indian commerce apps sell rather than educate. Insurance brokers are optimised for selling and say little about health. That gap is Nirog.com's opening.

## Patterns that win

- **Named, credentialed reviewers on every page.** Healthline, WebMD, Verywell and Harvard put the doctor's name, credentials and review date near the headline, and link to a reviewer-board page.
- **Public editorial, sourcing and advertising policies.** Every top publisher links to content-integrity and ad policies. Sites that do not, such as TheHealthSite, look weak next to them.
- **Visible evidence markers.** Medical News Today shows a citation count, Drugs.com names its data sources with dates, and Examine grades the evidence.
- **A tools hub as a traffic engine.** Calculators (BMI, BMR, due date, A1C), checkers and risk tests earn backlinks and repeat visits. Each one carries an explainer and links to related tools.
- **Short risk tests that end in a next step.** Diabetes.org's 60-second test and Sleep Foundation's quiz turn curiosity into an action or a lead.
- **Symptom triage with safe routing.** NHS 111, healthdirect and Cleveland Clinic route people to self-care, a GP or emergency care, and say plainly that they do not diagnose.
- **Multi-step lead wizards.** Policybazaar and ACKO ask one easy question per screen (members, ages, city) before asking for a phone number. Medical history comes after, to match plans.
- **Low-friction phone capture with consent.** Coverfox and Medanta collect a mobile number in one field, with an NDNC/TCPA-style consent checkbox and a call-back promise.
- **WhatsApp as a primary CTA in India.** Max, Medanta and Ditto put WhatsApp next to Call. Indian users expect it.
- **Anti-spam trust copy.** Ditto's "no spam, no salesmen" line and PharmEasy's dark-pattern declaration reduce form anxiety.
- **Social proof in local units.** Indian sites quote crore and lakh numbers, ratings, claims settled and NABH/JCI badges.
- **Segmented newsletters and gated lead magnets.** WebMD lets readers pick newsletter topics, and Harvard offers a free report for an email address.
- **Condition hubs.** Netmeds, Everyday Health and Policybazaar build hubs (diabetes, BP, seniors) that group articles, tools and a relevant offer.
- **Accessible, task-first design.** NHS and MedlinePlus use skip links, plain language, easy-read versions and strong contrast. NHS blue cards with bold headings scan well on mobile.
- **Donation modules with presets.** Mayo and diabetes.org show preset amounts, a monthly default and a personal-message hook.

## Feature checklist adopted for Nirog.com

### Content
- [ ] A–Z conditions library (English + Hindi), each page in a fixed template: overview, symptoms, causes, prevention, tests, when to see a doctor, FAQ
- [ ] Preventive-health hubs: diabetes, heart/BP, thyroid, PCOS, cancer screening, vaccination, senior care, mental health
- [ ] Easy-read summary box at the top of each article (60-word "in short")
- [ ] Myth vs fact series that grades home remedies and Ayurveda claims (Examine-style evidence grades)
- [ ] Seasonal alert strip: dengue, heatwave, AQI, flu, using official Indian sources
- [ ] Google Web Stories for top topics

### Tools
- [ ] BMI, BMR/calorie, ideal weight, waist-to-height and due-date calculators, each with unit toggle, result chart, explainer and formula
- [ ] Two-minute risk tests (Type 2 diabetes using IDRS, heart risk, sleep), each ending in a recommended check-up package
- [ ] Indian-food calorie lookup (roti, dal, biryani portions)
- [ ] Health insurance cover calculator (city tier, family size, age leading to a suggested sum insured)
- [ ] Vaccination and screening schedule by age and gender, downloadable as PDF

### Lead generation
- [ ] Multi-step insurance wizard: members, then ages, then city, then pre-existing conditions, then name and mobile, with a progress bar
- [ ] Check-up package finder: age, gender and city, then a matched package and a callback form
- [ ] Doctor-consult CTA as symptom tiles that open a pre-filled request
- [ ] Explicit consent checkbox (DPDP Act 2023), a "no spam" promise and a choice of call time
- [ ] Sticky mobile bar with Call, WhatsApp and Get advice buttons
- [ ] Gated lead magnet: "Family Preventive Health Planner" PDF for an email address

### Monetization
- [ ] AdSense in-content slots with fixed heights (to avoid layout shift), none above the fold on YMYL pages, and none inside tools
- [ ] Partner leads for insurers, labs and teleconsult platforms on a CPL basis, with a disclosed partner list
- [ ] Clearly labelled sponsored condition hubs, run under a published sponsorship policy
- [ ] Segmented newsletter that readers opt into by topic

### Trust & compliance
- [ ] "Medically reviewed by" (name, registration number, date) and "Fact checked by" on every page
- [ ] Reviewer board page with photos, qualifications and NMC/state registration
- [ ] Editorial, sourcing, advertising, corrections and AI-use policies, linked in every footer
- [ ] Inline citations, visible references count and "last updated" date
- [ ] Medical disclaimer, an emergency panel (112/108) on symptom pages, and an IRDAI disclosure on insurance pages
- [ ] Schema markup: MedicalWebPage, FAQPage, Organization, Person (reviewer), BreadcrumbList

### Design & UX
- [ ] Mobile-first static HTML, fast LCP, system or one web font, high-contrast palette
- [ ] Card grids with bold headings (NHS style), icon-led tool grid on the homepage
- [ ] English/हिन्दी toggle on every page, with hreflang tags
- [ ] Search-first hero with trending-topic chips
- [ ] Accessibility: skip link, ARIA labels, 16px+ body text, no autoplay

### Community
- [ ] Monthly contests: health quiz leaderboard, a 10k-steps challenge, a healthy-recipe contest (with published rules and prizes)
- [ ] Donations page with preset ₹ amounts, monthly option and transparent use of funds (e.g. free screening camps)
- [ ] Careers page: medical writers, reviewers, translators, with an application form

## Gaps Nirog.com can own

- **Trusted Hindi-first preventive content.** Hindi publishers (TheHealthSite, OnlyMyHealth) lack reviewer bylines and citations. Nirog can match Healthline-grade trust signals in Hindi and English.
- **Neutral preventive check-up guidance.** Labs and pharmacies push their own packages. Nobody explains which tests to take at which age, and why, before handing off to a provider.
- **Insurance explained by health need, not only by price.** Brokers compare premiums. Nirog can explain coverage by condition (diabetes, maternity, seniors) and then send a qualified lead.
- **Evidence grades for home remedies and Ayurveda.** Indian sites either promote remedies or ignore them. An Examine-style graded verdict fills a large gap in search results.
- **Indian-context tools.** Asian BMI cut-offs, IDRS diabetes risk, Indian-food calories and city-tier insurance cover are rare as free, standalone, ad-supported calculators.
- **India-specific seasonal and environmental health.** Heatwave, AQI, dengue and monsoon infection guidance, tied to official sources and refreshed each season.
- **Community-driven prevention.** None of the Indian sites reviewed runs contests, challenges and donation-funded screening camps together. This builds loyalty and gets press coverage.
- **Transparent lead gen.** A public partner list, a "no spam" promise and DPDP-compliant consent would set Nirog apart from aggressive broker call flows.
